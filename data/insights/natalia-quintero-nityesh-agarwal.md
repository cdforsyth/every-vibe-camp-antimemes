## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Non-Worth Tasks Into Trivial Ones

**INSIGHT:** The real value of AI automation isn't making hard tasks easy - it's making "not-worth-it" tasks so trivial that you actually do them, like creating a polished deck just to demo a plugin.

**CATEGORY:** Antimeme

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Specificity: 5
- Surprise: 4
- Shareability: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Cognitive

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** People frame AI value as "making hard things easy" - the "trivializing medium things" frame is less dramatic but more practically useful
- **Implementation Antimeme:** The tasks that become trivial are the ones you wouldn't have done at all, so you don't notice the absence

**Why it resists spreading:**
- "Made my hard task easier" is a better story than "I did a thing I wouldn't have bothered with"
- The counterfactual (not doing the task at all) is invisible
- People want to talk about big productivity gains, not trivial tasks becoming even more trivial
- "I made a demo deck" sounds less impressive than "I automated my workflow"

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The narrative of heroic productivity gains
- The assumption that AI should focus on your hardest problems

**Verification:**
> "I am using AI to turn producing not worth a task into trivial ones. So like this deck is the example of that. Like I just generated this deck to show you what this plugin does."

### 2. Blueprint → Visual Direction → Generation

**INSIGHT:** Separate presentation creation into three distinct phases: content planning (blueprint), visual design decisions (visual direction), then final generation - working on the plan before the artifact.

**CATEGORY:** Workflow

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Specificity: 5
- Surprise: 3
- Shareability: 3

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 7

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Most people want to go straight to the final artifact; the intermediate blueprint/visual direction steps feel like overhead

**Why it resists spreading:**
- The temptation to skip to "just make the deck" is strong
- Planning documents feel like bureaucracy
- The value of separate phases isn't obvious until you've tried one-shot generation and hit quality issues

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The satisfaction of immediate output
- The illusion that one-shot generation should just work

**Verification:**
> "There's a generate blueprint command included in this plugin, which actually uses whatever we discussed above to generate a rough markdown file which discusses the entire Blueprint... And then there's another command called visual direction."

### 4. Designer as Curation Layer, Not Production

**INSIGHT:** In AI-augmented workflows, designers shift from producing initial artifacts to curating and polishing AI-generated drafts - their role becomes quality control and taste, not production.

**CATEGORY:** Workflow

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 4
- Specificity: 4
- Surprise: 4
- Shareability: 4

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Social / Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 7

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Social Antimeme:** Designers may feel threatened by being repositioned as "editors" rather than "creators"
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Integrating AI-first workflow with human design review requires process changes

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Designers invested years in production skills, not curation skills
- "Polishing AI output" sounds less prestigious than "creating from scratch"
- Organizational structures assume designers produce, not review
- The handoff between AI generation and human polish isn't standardized

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The designer identity as primary creator
- Existing production-focused workflows

**Verification:**
> "We have an incredibly talented design team so these version of the slides are not the final version that our clients would get... the next version of this for us is like how do we integrate our workflow with our design team's workflow."

### 5. Content One-Shot More Valuable Than Design One-Shot

**INSIGHT:** Being close to one-shotting content (the intellectual work of what to say) is more valuable than one-shotting design (how it looks) - focus automation efforts on the harder cognitive work.

**CATEGORY:** Antimeme

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 3
- Specificity: 4
- Surprise: 4
- Shareability: 3

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Cognitive

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 6

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** People often focus on visual polish (more visible) over content quality (more valuable)
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Design issues are easier to see and complain about than content issues

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Visual imperfections are immediately obvious
- "The design isn't quite right" is easier feedback than "the content structure is wrong"
- The hardest work (figuring out what to say) is invisible
- Design polish is what makes demos look good

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The focus on visual perfection
- The assumption that automation should fully replace human work

**Verification:**
> "There's like the content piece and I feel like we're pretty close to one-shotting like the content that is most relevant to clients which is maybe the most valuable part of this."

## Stage 3: Summary

**Top 3 by Diagnostic Value:**
1. **Non-Worth Tasks Into Trivial Ones** (Antimeme) - The real AI value is trivializing medium tasks so you do them - Score: 8
2. **Blueprint → Visual Direction → Generation** (Workflow) - Three-phase process for quality presentations - Score: 7
3. **600-Line Style Guide Upfront Investment** (Tactic) - Document design rules thoroughly upfront - Score: 7

**Most Shareable (Twitter-ready):**
1. **Non-Worth Tasks Into Trivial Ones** - Reframes the AI productivity conversation in a counterintuitive way. The demo deck example is concrete and memorable.
2. **Designer as Curation Layer** - Speaks to the anxiety around AI replacing creative workers while offering a more nuanced view. Would spark discussion.

**Most Actionable:**
1. **Blueprint → Visual Direction → Generation** - Next time you're using AI for presentations, explicitly create a blueprint markdown file before generating visuals

**Pattern Across Insights:**
Natalia and Nityesh's insights cluster around the theme of structured investment paying off in speed. The 600-line style guide, the three-phase workflow, the upfront blueprint - all represent deliberate slowing down to enable faster execution later. The antimemetic insight about "non-worth tasks becoming trivial" reframes AI value from heroic productivity to practical optionality. The designer-as-curation insight acknowledges that AI doesn't replace humans but shifts where human judgment is most valuable. The common thread: AI automation works best when you invest upfront in structure and accept that human polish still matters.
