## Stage 2: Antimemetic Properties Analysis

### 1. Claude Project Beat the Custom App

**INSIGHT:** After failing to build a custom AI editing app, Every discovered that a simple Claude Project with custom instructions worked better and more reliably - because Anthropic maintains Claude, not you.

**CATEGORY:** Antimeme

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Ignoring
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Vertigo

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Specificity: 5
- Surprise: 5
- Shareability: 5

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Psychological / Social

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 8

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** Building custom apps feels more impressive and "real" than configuring existing tools
- **Social Antimeme:** Saying "I built an app" sounds better than "I set up a Claude Project"
- **Implementation Antimeme:** The sunk cost of building makes it hard to admit the simpler solution works better

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Custom apps feel like "real" building; Claude Projects feel like configuration
- You want credit for the work you did, not the work you avoided
- Admitting failure is uncomfortable, especially publicly
- The industry narrative celebrates building, not buying/configuring

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The satisfaction of building your own thing
- The narrative of technical accomplishment
- The sunk cost of previous building efforts

**Verification:**
> "I failed building this last year and we realized that a Claude project just set up with custom instructions and some documentation worked for editing our pieces as well as what I was building and more reliably because it wasn't built on an app that I had to maintain."

### 2. Audit First When Lost

**INSIGHT:** When completely stuck on a codebase, use compound engineering to run an audit and identify improvement opportunities - the audit output becomes your guide for what to do next.

**CATEGORY:** Workflow

**CLASSIFICATION:** Diagnostic

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 5
- Specificity: 5
- Surprise: 3
- Shareability: 3

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 7

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Implementation Antimeme:** When stuck, the instinct is to keep trying specific fixes, not step back and audit

**Why it resists spreading:**
- When frustrated, you want to take action, not do analysis
- Audit feels like admitting you don't know what's wrong
- The output might tell you things you don't want to hear
- It requires plugins/setup most people don't have

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The hope that the next random attempt will work
- The illusion that you understand the codebase

**Verification:**
> "I'll get stuck and I will ask the agent when I have no idea what's going on to do an audit through compound engineering... And I was like, oh yeah, that looks good. Let's go in that direction."

### 3. ELI5 to Level Up

**INSIGHT:** After getting technical output you don't understand, ask Claude to "explain these changes like I'm five" - this repeated practice builds your understanding of terminology and concepts over time.

- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**

- Specificity: 5
- Surprise: 2
- Shareability: 3

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Psychological

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 6

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Psychological Antimeme:** Asking for ELI5 feels embarrassing, like admitting ignorance

**Why it resists spreading:**
- Pride makes you want to figure it out yourself
- "Like I'm five" feels undignified
- The instinct is to pretend you understand and move on
- Non-coders might not realize this is a valid learning strategy

**What's lost by accepting it:**

**Verification:**

### 6. Track Accept/Reject for Learning

**INSIGHT:** The dream is visibility into when AI feedback gets accepted vs rejected - tracking this over time reveals when AI is valuable and when human input wins, improving the system.

**CATEGORY:** Antimeme

**CLASSIFICATION:** Frontier

**QUICK TEST:**
- Behavior change possible? Y
- Missing vs. ignoring? Missing
- Recognition vs. vertigo? Recognition

**SCORES:**
- Actionability: 3
- Specificity: 4
- Surprise: 4
- Shareability: 3

**RESISTANCE TYPE:** Implementation

**DIAGNOSTIC VALUE:** 7

**Antimemetic Properties:**
- **Implementation Antimeme:** Most AI tools don't track accept/reject rates; implementing this requires custom work
- **Cognitive Antimeme:** People assume AI feedback quality is binary (good/bad) not measurable

**Why it resists spreading:**
- No off-the-shelf tools for this tracking
- Requires discipline to log decisions
- The data might reveal uncomfortable truths about AI's actual value
- People just want to get work done, not measure it

**What's lost by accepting it:**
- The simplicity of just accepting/rejecting without tracking
- The assumption that you can evaluate AI "by feel"

**Verification:**
> "Something that we're looking to do from an editorial perspective is just get more visibility into when we accept the AI's recommendations, when we reject them, when they're more valuable, when the human input is more valuable."

**Pattern Across Insights:**
Katie's insights cluster around the theme of productive humility - being honest about what you don't know and finding strategies to work effectively anyway. The Claude Project insight shows that admitting failure can lead to better solutions. The ELI5 tactic and skim-like-articles approach both work around, rather than solve, knowledge gaps. The Theranos confession normalizes hitting limits. The accept/reject tracking idea shows curiosity about where AI actually adds value. The common thread: non-technical builders can be highly effective if they're honest about limitations and strategic about working within them.
